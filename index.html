Quest log

THE LOOP

Every pile on the map is a level. The swarm runs the same four moves on each one, and the order matters: nothing is collected that can't be sorted, and nothing is sorted that can't be sold.

STEP 1

SCOUT

Drones and public data map every dump, ditch, bin and landfill. Each pile gets a tile with an estimated mass and material mix.

STEP 2

COLLECT

Hauler bots and trucks run routes the planner sets, by the tonne. Wet organics move first, before they rot into methane.

STEP 3

SEGREGATE

Sorter bots use vision, near-infrared and X-ray to split by material, not category. PET from HDPE. Copper from brass. Magnet from casing.

STEP 4

TRANSFORM & SELL

Modular refiners turn each stream into a graded product. A trading agent lists it, prices it and ships it to the buyer.

Research

ROBOTS THAT DESIGN ROBOTS

The swarm logs every item it fails to sort or refine. The lab turns that log into the next bot.

lab@anygarbs:~$ tail failures.log

MISS tile 41-07 · laminated tetra-pak · sorter confidence 0.31 · 1.8 t/week

MISS tile 12-22 · lithium pouch cells in bag mix · hauler halted (thermal) · 0.4 t/week

MISS tile 08-03 · fibreglass boat hull · no refiner module · 6.0 t (one-off)

lab@anygarbs:~$ propose --stream tetra-pak --target "PE-Al split, pulp out"

OK candidate: hydrapulper module v3 + eddy-current stage · sim yield 91 % · print parts: 14 · build: 9 days

PROPOSE

An AI design agent drafts a gripper, sensor head or refiner module for the stream the swarm keeps missing. Input: the failure log, plus real scans of the pile.

SIMULATE

The candidate is run against recorded belt footage and digital twins of the piles. Yield, energy per tonne and jam rate decide if it gets built.

FIELD TEST

Parts are printed and machined, fitted to one bot, and run on a live route for two weeks. What works ships to every swarm as a firmware and parts kit.
